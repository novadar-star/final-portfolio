# FINAL CREATIVE DIRECTION — MAKE THIS FEEL LIKE NOVA

I want to change the creative direction of the portfolio.

After seeing the current implementation, I realized the goal is NOT simply to make a “less AI-looking developer portfolio.”

The goal is much more specific:

## I want this website to feel like a personal universe.

Look at the creative philosophy behind Miffy Wang's portfolio at:

https://miffywang.com/

DO NOT copy her visual design.

DO NOT copy her typography, colors, animations, layout, illustrations, or exact section names.

Instead, study the underlying principle:

> Her website feels like a collection of Miffy herself, rather than a website designed to market Miffy.

That is what I want.

---

# THE NEW NORTH STAR

The site should make someone feel:

> “I found Nova's corner of the internet.”

Not:

> “I found a very well-designed CS student's portfolio.”

And definitely not:

> “I found an AI automation portfolio template.”

My portfolio is allowed to be:

* technical
* strange
* playful
* imperfect
* personal
* editorial
* internet-y
* visually experimental

as long as it feels intentional.

---

# IMPORTANT: STOP TRYING TO EXPLAIN MY BRAND

Do not create sections whose primary purpose is explaining:

* my positioning
* my capabilities
* my professional identity
* my personality
* my values

The current site sometimes feels like it is trying to answer every question a recruiter could possibly have.

Don't.

Let the work answer those questions.

If someone sees:

* an n8n workflow
* a custom JSON parser
* an AI application
* AWS/community work
* a hackathon project
* a weird side experiment

they already understand a lot about me.

---

# THE WEBSITE SHOULD FEEL CURATED, NOT COMPLETE

I do NOT want a database of everything I've ever done.

I want a carefully selected collection of things that reveal who I am.

Think:

> “these are some things Nova has made / cared about / gotten obsessed with.”

not:

> “here is Nova's complete professional history.”

The site should have a point of view.

---

# HOMEPAGE PHILOSOPHY

Use this as the conceptual structure:

## NOVA

My identity.

A short, human introduction.

Something like:

> Darla “Nova” Sumanting
> CS student, automation builder, cloud community person

Then one short sentence that sounds like me.

Do NOT make this a giant professional headline.

Do NOT use:

> AI Automation Specialist

> Full-Stack Developer

> Cloud & AI Engineer

unless genuinely necessary elsewhere.

---

## THINGS I'VE MADE

This is the main body of the site.

Projects should feel like artifacts rather than cards.

Do NOT make every project visually identical.

Different projects can have different compositions.

For example:

* one large image
* one workflow diagram
* one text-heavy artifact
* one tiny experiment
* one interactive element
* one screenshot with annotations

The visual treatment should respond to the project.

Not the other way around.

The project list itself should feel like a collection.

---

# PROJECT STORYTELLING

The writing should stay extremely specific.

The current AI job scraper description is a good example of the direction:

> “the AI kept returning malformed JSON, so i wrote a parser…”

KEEP THIS TYPE OF WRITING.

It is much stronger than generic portfolio copy.

Whenever possible, explain:

* what made me build it
* what went wrong
* what I changed
* what surprised me
* what I learned

through natural writing.

Do not turn every project into:

Problem → Solution → Impact → Stack.

That is a case-study template.

The underlying information can exist in the content model, but the presentation should feel like someone telling me about something they actually built.

---

# SLICES OF NOVA

I want a section inspired by the conceptual role of Miffy's “SLICES OF LIFE.”

This section is extremely important.

It is where things that don't fit neatly into “developer portfolio” are allowed to exist.

Possible slices:

* AWS Student Builder Group / community
* hackathons
* design
* anime / manga
* fanfiction
* experiments
* random things I've become obsessed with
* other creative work

Do NOT turn these into generic cards.

This should feel like fragments of a person.

The exact content can evolve later.

---

# SKILL / TOOL INVENTORY

KEEP the skill wall.

It is relevant to my portfolio.

But it should feel like:

> “things I keep reaching for”

rather than:

> “my technical competencies.”

Possible framing:

> tools i keep reaching for

or another phrase that feels natural.

Keep the actual tools because they provide useful technical context.

Group them naturally:

automation
AI
cloud
code
other

Do not give every technology an oversized logo.

Do not create progress bars.

Do not imply expertise levels unless I explicitly provide them.

The projects should provide the evidence.

The tool section provides the quick reference.

---

# EXPERIENCE

Keep professional/leadership experience.

But make it compact.

I do not want a full resume recreated on the homepage.

Experience should provide context:

* internship
* AWS community leadership
* relevant organizations
* notable roles

If someone wants the complete chronology, they can open my resume.

---

# ABOUT

The About section should feel like discovering another side of me.

Do not write a traditional biography.

Use a small amount of prose.

Mention real things.

Anime/manga.

Fanfiction.

Design.

Curiosity.

Being a try-hard.

Whatever is genuinely me.

The important part:

DO NOT TRY TO MAKE THESE DETAILS SOUND QUIRKY.

They are not “quirky personal branding.”

They are simply things I like.

That distinction is important.

---

# VISUAL IDENTITY

I want a visual language that feels like it belongs to Nova.

Do NOT use a generic “creative developer” visual language.

Do NOT imitate Miffy.

Instead, derive visual ideas from my actual world:

* workflows
* diagrams
* notes
* arrows
* connections
* screenshots
* browser-like artifacts
* little observations
* annotations
* imperfect structures
* technical diagrams
* internet culture

But do not literally decorate everything with workflow arrows.

The visual language should emerge naturally.

---

# ALLOW DIFFERENT PARTS OF THE SITE TO HAVE DIFFERENT RHYTHMS

This is important.

Do not make every section:

eyebrow
big heading
paragraph
cards

repeat.

Allow:

* a huge project image
* then a tiny text fragment
* then a dense technical list
* then lots of whitespace
* then an image
* then a small personal note
* then a project

The site should breathe.

---

# TYPOGRAPHY

Typography should feel distinctive, but NOT “design portfolio trendy.”

Do not overuse:

* uppercase micro-labels
* monospace
* huge bold sans-serif
* excessive italics
* artificial editorial typography

Typography should feel like part of my personality rather than a design trick.

---

# COLOR

Do not default to:

black + white + neon green

or

beige + rust + editorial green

just because those are currently popular portfolio aesthetics.

Choose a palette that feels native to the content and the personality.

It can be simple.

It can even be slightly unexpected.

The goal is recognizability, not trendiness.

---

# INTERACTION

One or two unusual interactions are enough.

The interaction should feel like something Nova would have put on her website.

Not something a UI library happened to make possible.

Avoid:

* cursor effects
* text scrambling
* excessive parallax
* scroll animations everywhere
* fake terminals
* typing effects
* floating blobs

If an interaction does not reveal something about the content, remove it.

---

# MAINTAINABILITY

This principle does NOT change.

The website must still work beautifully without updates for 6+ months.

Do not depend on:

* current status
* latest project
* current obsession
* weekly notes
* timestamps
* “currently building”

The site should be evergreen.

New projects should be added through content/data only.

---

# MOST IMPORTANT DESIGN RULE

Do not try to make the website look “unique.”

That usually creates another aesthetic template.

Instead:

## Make the website contain things that only Nova would have.

The uniqueness should come from:

* my projects
* my writing
* my weird interests
* my technical decisions
* my artifacts
* my sense of humor
* my community work
* my visual choices

The design should amplify those things.

It should not manufacture uniqueness around them.

---

# FINAL TEST

Before considering the implementation finished, ask:

### If I removed my name, could this website belong to another AI automation student?

If yes → it is still too generic.

### Does every section exist because Nova actually has something to say/show there?

If no → remove it.

### Does the site feel like a person curated it?

If no → loosen the system.

### Does the site feel like a portfolio template?

If yes → break the template.

### Does the site feel like an internet corner belonging to one specific person?

If yes → stop.

Do not add more polish.

Do not add more sections.

Do not add more effects.

Do not optimize it into a corporate portfolio.

The goal is not perfection.

The goal is recognition.

Someone should be able to visit this site and later see a screenshot of it and immediately know:

> “oh, that's Nova's site.”
